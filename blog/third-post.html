BP03: Open Source Reflection

November 19, 2026

Introduction: Looking Forward and Looking Back

We are now very close to the end of the semester, so moving forward, I need to make sure I am able to finish the remaining work for BCSR and CAS that I have. To do this, I am going to look back at how I have worked so far and what needs to be improved.

Risk 1: Logistical Challenges

One logistical challenge that coud prevent me from finishing strong is getting lost in so many different tasks during the end of semester rush. I am going to have to be able to balance three final projects that I am currently working on, as well as my open source contributions, and my everyday responsibilities to my family. I have already seen how pull requests can stay open for weeks. During my sprint retrospective, I was able to see that there are alot of pull requests that are not merged yet. To manage this risk, I will break big story point contributions into smaller tasks. Then, I will choose the top priority tasks to work on every coding session I have. I will also make sure to communicate with my team members about what I am working on and what I need help with. As well as letting them know if they could review my work.

Risk 2: Interpersonal Challenges

An interpersonal risk that I can run into is miscommunication or slow feedback when communicating with my teammates. Part of working on an open source repo is you have to depend on others for reviews and merges. Which it is my responsibility to make sure I am communicating clearly and effectively. But, if there is slow reviews and then the feedback comes back with different expecations then what I was expecting, then it can slow down my progress. I have already spent time reviewing my peers work and given that I also created our sprint retrospective, I was able to see how much smoother things can go when there is clear communication and expectations. To reduce this risk, I will make sure to be communicating with the maintainers of the repo I work on and making clear of their expectations. I already saw how effective this is as I went to go speak to Imran about the pull request that I am currently working on with Alpha, and it was so helpful to understand what the expectations were and what I needed to do to get it merged. I will also make sure to check in with my team more often instead of waiting until deadlines are close.

Responsibility to the Users

Faculty, Code owners, maintainers and future developers depend on the work I am contributing to on BCSR and CAS. It is expected for these repos to work reliably. Small bugs and issues can still make a big impact on the user experience. Because these applications will continue to be used and maintained after the semester ends, how I end the semester matters. So far, what I have learned about open-source maintenance, documentation, and sustainability shapes how I prioritize my remaining work: Maintenance: Finishing and cleaning up open PRs is more helpful than starting lots of new issues that might remain unfinished. Documentation: Clear notes and easy-to-read code help teammates and future contributors understand changes without guessing or a lot of back and forth about trying to understand what you did. Sustainability: Simple, clean changes are much easier for future developers to maintain over time. Because of these responsibilities, I am focusing on wrapping up my current PRs cleanly, keeping my code and explanations easy to follow, and prioritizing reliable fixes .

What Success Looks Like

For me, success by the end of the semester means that I hit my story point forecast goal, finish my remaining PRs, communicate with my teammates and help them if they have any issues, need help, or need a review. My progress so far shows that I am able to identify and create issues like issue 144 and also work on and actually get issues merged into development. (Ex. PR 114, PR 151, PR 464). My progress also shows that I can support my peers through code reviews. Ending the semester with clean,merged work and good communication with my team will show that I contributed meaningfully.

Progress Update

Currently, I still only have 5 earned story points. This however is because I have two open pull requests. And one of them, I estimated to be worth 8 story points. So given that it is a bigger issue, means I have not made much progress on my story point goal since the last blog. I also had to catch up on my other final project, which gave me less time to work on my issues.

Outside of resolved issues, I created the retrospective presentation for my group. This was important because the retrospective gave my team space to talk about what is working, what is slowing us down, and how to improve our workflow together.
